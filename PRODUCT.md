# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Two audiences share the product with no explicit priority between them. The first is individuals sending emotionally significant messages: a letter to a loved one, a note to their own future self, a reminder tied to a date that matters. The second is privacy-conscious users protecting sensitive material for a dead-man's-switch scenario: credentials, important data, or instructions that should only surface if something happens to them. Both arrive through the same flow (compose, encrypt, schedule) and the product doesn't assume one over the other in any given session.

## Product Purpose

Envault encrypts a message in the browser and schedules it for delivery at an exact future moment, from a day out to a decade out. It exists so that time-sensitive or emotionally weighty communication doesn't depend on the sender staying available, remembering, or trusting a third party to hold the plaintext. Success is a feeling, not a metric: the sender walks away calm because the delivery will happen exactly when promised, and nobody but the recipient, not even Envault, can ever read what was sent.

## Positioning

End-to-end encryption paired with precise time-based delivery: the message is unreadable to anyone but the recipient, and it arrives exactly when scheduled, not roughly, not "eventually."

## Brand Personality

Calm, reliable, private. The tone should read like a service built for personal, sometimes vulnerable moments, never like a security vendor showing off. The visual direction the founder is pulling toward: a dark backdrop with warm amber-to-crimson gradients and glow, replacing the current cool purple accent. That warmth is meant to soften the "vault" seriousness without losing the sense of care and precision.

## Anti-references

The generic "SaaS corporate" look: flat gradients, identical feature cards, stock-photo business tone. Envault is personal infrastructure, not a B2B dashboard, and should never read like one.

## Design Principles

Calm confidence over urgency: the product reassures rather than hypes; nothing should ever feel like it's pushing the user to act faster than the moment calls for.

Precision is the trust signal: any UI touching time, delivery state, or encryption status must be exact and legible, never vague copy standing in for a real guarantee.

Privacy is structural, not decorative: end-to-end encryption is a quiet guarantee built into the flow, not a badge repeated on every screen.

One product, one path: the home page is the front door of the same product, not a separate marketing site, so it carries the same visual system as the dashboard and app screens.

Reject corporate SaaS scaffolding: no generic gradients, identical card grids, or eyebrow labels that could belong to any B2B tool.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal requirements (no stated WCAG level). Follow standard good practice: sufficient contrast and a `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback for any animation.
